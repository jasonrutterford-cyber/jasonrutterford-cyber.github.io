## The short version

Most of my career has been inside large organisations rather than at an agency: telecoms, marketing, oil and gas, commercial and supply chain, in the UK and now the UAE. I've sat on the client side of the table when the big budgets get committed, and I've seen how often they get committed to the wrong problem.

That's the thing I'm useful for. Before an organisation spends real money, I help its leaders work out what the problem actually is, and what's worth investing in. Then they can brief the agencies and systems integrators properly, or decide not to.

## The breadth of the work

Four jobs, usually at the same time.

- **Finding out what's actually going on.** Research with customers and colleagues, stakeholder interviews, the data already lying around, and the synthesis that turns it into a problem statement people can agree on. Done in the time a leader actually has, not as a research phase.
- **Product and investment strategy.** Working out what an organisation should build, buy, fix or stop, and in what order. The business case, the outcomes it will be judged on, and what to do first. Often the answer is a smaller project than the one that was asked for.
- **Service and business process design.** The whole service, not the screen: the process behind it, the hand-offs between teams, the policies, the data and systems it touches, and the people who run it day to day. Blueprints and future-state journeys that delivery teams and agencies can build from, with the operations side designed in rather than discovered later.
- **Experience architecture and leadership.** The end-to-end experience across customer, citizen and colleague journeys; the standards and governance that keep it coherent; and building experience functions from the ground up, with the credibility that gets design into the room where decisions are made.

AI runs through all four: as the way the work gets done faster, and increasingly as a participant in the service being designed.

## What "experience" means here

Not just UX. Customer experience, service design, business process design, product strategy and colleague experience, using the playbooks from UX and design thinking, with AI where it genuinely speeds things up. The same methods work on problems that have nothing to do with screens: a supply chain hand-off, an approval that takes three weeks, a policy nobody can explain.

## How I work

One person, not a team. Quiet, pragmatic and honest. I'd rather say the true thing plainly than make it sound clever. If I don't think you have the problem you think you have, I'll say so.
