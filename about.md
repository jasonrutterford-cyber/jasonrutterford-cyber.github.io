## The short version

Most of my career has been inside large organisations rather than at an agency: telecoms, marketing, oil and gas, commercial and supply chain, in the UK and now the UAE. I've sat on the client side of the table when the big budgets get committed, and I've seen how often they get committed to the wrong problem.

That's the thing I'm useful for. Before an organisation spends real money, I help its leaders work out what the problem actually is, and what's worth investing in. Then they can brief the agencies and systems integrators properly, or decide not to.

## The breadth of the work

Three jobs, usually at the same time.

- **Discovery.** Finding out what is actually going on: research with customers and colleagues, journey and lifecycle mapping, stakeholder interviews, and the synthesis that turns all of it into a problem statement people can agree on.
- **Design strategy and architecture.** Deciding what the experience should be end to end, where to invest and in what order. Service design, business process design and experience architecture across customer, citizen and colleague journeys. The blueprint the delivery teams and agencies build from.
- **Leadership.** Building experience functions from the ground up: the team, the operating model, the standards, the service offering, and the credibility with executives that gets design into the room where decisions are made.

## What "experience" means here

Not just UX. Customer experience, service design, business process design and colleague experience, using the playbooks from UX and design thinking, with AI where it genuinely speeds things up. The same methods work on problems that have nothing to do with screens.

## How I work

One person, not a team. Quiet, pragmatic and honest. I'd rather say the true thing plainly than make it sound clever. If I don't think you have the problem you think you have, I'll say so.

Everything here is my own view. I write about patterns and principles, not about any employer's specifics, past or present.
