Transforming a fragmented digital ecosystem

One front door for a portfolio of technical applications.

Problem
Many products, no common experience.
My role
Head of Experience, Platform and AI. I led the product and experience strategy.
What I did
Turned research into a set of prioritised bets.
Delivered
Fewer portals, unified help and support, and modelling software that loads in under 90 seconds, down from 15 minutes.
The problem

Lots of products. No shared experience.

No personalisation
Nothing recognised who you were.
No reason to return
Customers came for one task and left.
Work stopped at the edges
Workflows ended at product boundaries.
The insight

People don't want a portal. They want to orient, work and recharge.

Stage 1
Orient
Launch, track and communicate.
Stage 2
Work
Share, assign and collect.
Stage 3
Recharge
Review, explore and learn.
The strategy

Bets, not a backlog.

Problem space
User needs
Hypotheses
Bets
Measures
Every bet named what it assumed and what would prove it.
How I led it
1Interviewed leaders across three business domains
2Tested concepts with customers
3Defined two user archetypes
4Scored each bet with product leaders
The value it was built for
Adoption
Fewer clicks from entry to work.
Retention
More successful return visits.
Efficiency
Faster loading, fewer errors.
What changed: fewer portals, one help and support service, and load time for heavy graphical modelling software cut from 15 minutes to under 90 seconds.
What I learned

Frame strategy as bets. People commit to a test faster than a roadmap.
